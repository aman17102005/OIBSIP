A Tribute

Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

The boy from Rameswaram who taught a nation to dream with its eyes open.

15 October 1931 — 27 July 2015

A Life of Purpose

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born in Rameswaram, a small island town in Tamil Nadu, into a family that had very little money but plenty of values. As a young boy he sold newspapers before school to help at home, and he spent his evenings watching birds fly over the sea, wondering how they stayed in the air. That simple curiosity about flight stayed with him for the rest of his life.

He studied physics and then aerospace engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology. After graduating, he began work as a scientist in the defence research system and later moved to the Indian Space Research Organisation. There he led the team that built SLV-3, India's first home-made satellite launch vehicle, which put the Rohini satellite into orbit in 1980. It was the moment India joined the small group of nations that could reach space on their own.

Back in defence research, he headed the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme, which produced missiles such as Agni and Prithvi and earned him the name "Missile Man of India". In 1998 he played a central role in the Pokhran-II nuclear tests. Throughout this work he was known for giving credit to his team, taking the blame himself when things went wrong, and treating every junior engineer as a future leader.

In 2002 he became the 11th President of India. People called him the "People's President" because he opened the doors of Rashtrapati Bhavan to ordinary citizens, answered thousands of emails from students, and travelled across the country to speak in schools and colleges. Even after his term ended he never stopped teaching. He passed away on 27 July 2015 while giving a lecture to students in Shillong, doing exactly what he loved most.

Key Moments

  1. 1931

    Born on 15 October in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu.

  2. 1960

    Graduates in aerospace engineering and joins defence research (DRDO) as a scientist.

  3. 1969

    Moves to ISRO and becomes project director of India's first satellite launch vehicle.

  4. 1980

    SLV-3 places the Rohini satellite in orbit — India reaches space on its own.

  5. 1981

    Awarded the Padma Bhushan; the Padma Vibhushan follows in 1990.

  6. 1997

    Receives the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour.

  7. 1998

    Plays a key role in the Pokhran-II nuclear tests.

  8. 1999

    Publishes his autobiography Wings of Fire, co-written with Arun Tiwari.

  9. 2002

    Sworn in as the 11th President of India, serving until 2007.

  10. 2015

    Passes away on 27 July while lecturing to students at IIM Shillong.
